**OPUS23**  


## The scientific goal of the project:

**description of the problem to be solved**  
This project presumes that changes in oxygen partial pressure (pO2) in tumor tissues following
anti-cancer therapy may provide a key answer to the poor prognosis of pancreatic cancer. The fundaments
for this hypothesis are related to a variety of biological phenomena, all related to tissue hypoxia:
• unique pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC) microenvironment,
• a strong relationship between hypoxia and tumor invasiveness and resistance,
• hypoxia-induced Cancer Steam Cells (CSC) phenotype with higher migration capacity,
• induction of angiogenesis in a tumor can promote tumor growth,
• significant heterogeneity of pO2 levels after various treatments,
• role of oxygen in energy production in cells (needed for, e.g., proliferation),
• hypoxia mediates chemoresistance and stimulates multidrug resistance.
The above phenomena will be addressed in the current proposal and will be tested in the context
of pancreatic cancer. What is more, there is no comprehensive research on the role of pO2 in PDAC and
the possibilities of predicting treatment success based on changes in PDAC oxygenation level. Three
different orthotopic mice models (murine, human, and murine spontaneous PDAC) will be used to verify
the central hypothesis. Complementary oxygen measurement techniques will be applied to test early and
late oxygenation changes after different chemotherapy treatments and surgery. Electron Paramagnetic
Resonance (EPR) will be used to directly measure tissue oxygenation non-invasively, ultrasonography
(US) will be used to study tissue perfusion and photoacoustic imaging (PAI) will allow us to measure
hemoglobin saturation and collagen concentration in vivo.

**research questions and hypotheses**  
As the result of the project, we would be able to resolve the question – is the increase of
pancreatic tumor oxygenation after anti-tumor therapy possible? And further, if it is a sign of cure
or an indication of a progressive neoplastic disease?
We hypothesize that knowledge about PDAC oxygenation can be successfully used to guide
anti-tumor treatment for an individual mouse.
To answer the above questions with this proposal, specific goals were designed:
G1: Investigate changes in oxygenation, perfusion, functional vasculature, collagen concentration, and
hemoglobin saturation in PDAC tumors in mice models after chemotherapy and surgery.
G2: Register multimodal images and select the most important parameters to predict therapy outcomes.
G3: Correlate biological information like DNA damage, CSC presence, angiogenesis, and metastatic
potential from tumor biopsy with information collected during multimodal imaging of this area.
G4: Optimize therapy of spontaneous PDAC in mice based on physiological imaging.


## Relevance of the project:
Oxygenation is a significant component of the tissue microenvironment and metabolism. Oxygen is important to produce energy effectively by cells, allowing valuable cell functions
and having a high proliferation rate. However, too much oxygen can induce severe tissue damage due to
reactive oxygen species (ROS) production. Additionally, oxygen deficiency (below physiological levels)
is associated with pathological physiology and a hypoxic niche in tumors and probably CSCs niche.
